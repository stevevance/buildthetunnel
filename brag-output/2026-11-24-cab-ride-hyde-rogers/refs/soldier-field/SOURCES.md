# Soldier Field: reference images and sources

`assets/soldier-field.js` draws Holabird & Roche's 1924 colonnades, pavilions and old outer walls with the 2003 seating bowl set inside them, in the stadium's own frame (the field turned 4.2 degrees). The footprints come from OpenStreetMap's stadium outline and its parts (the colonnades and pavilions, the old south wall, the north extensions, the bowl and the pitch), which agree with the LiDAR to a meter or two. The heights and the bowl's shape come from Cook County's 2022 LiDAR (the surface model less the bare-earth model, street grade 181.0 m NAVD88), sampled on a 0.4-0.55 m grid in the stadium's frame and cut into sections across and along the field: the colonnades' 28.0 m cornice, the pavilions' 30.2 m ridges, the east glass building's 40.2-40.5 m roof, the west grandstand's rim and the slope of every stand. The architecture (22 Doric columns a row, the pediments and tile roofs, the saucer's silver rim, dark soffit, inclined glass wall and struts, the white frames on top) was drawn from openly licensed photographs on Wikimedia Commons and from the National Park Service's and Blair Kamin's descriptions.

Everything here is openly licensed (Creative Commons, public domain) or our own analysis, so all of it is committed to the public repository; this model has no `private/` files.

## Other sources the module cites (not saved as files)

- OpenStreetMap (Overpass through the Chicago Cityscape CLI, November 2026): way 24587933 and the parts listed in the module's header. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL.
- Cook County 2022 LiDAR, Illinois State Geological Survey: https://data.isgs.illinois.edu/arcgis/rest/services/Elevation/IL_Cook_DSM_2022/ImageServer and .../IL_Cook_DTM_2022/ImageServer.
- National Park Service, "Grant Park Stadium (Soldier Field)": the colonnades "formed by a double row of 32 columns"; the bowl "cantilevered and asymmetrical", its skyboxes rising "four stories beyond the original colonnades on the east side", its "curving steel and glass skin".
- Blair Kamin (quoted on Northwestern's Atlas blog, 2015): "the bulbous west grandstand that weighs down brutally on Soldier Field's once-proud columns" and the east "wall of 133 glass-clad luxury suites".
